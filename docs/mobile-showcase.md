# Mobile corridor and plate

The mobile workspace lets a visitor explore a genomics workflow and inspect result histories with a few taps. Its default Dark mode preserves the navy canvas, colored corridor lanes, upright paper cards, luminous wells, and result discs from the desktop application. Light and High contrast share the same geometry, interactions and record meaning through the [theme contract](theme-contract.md). Example data supports a portfolio demonstration; the desktop workspace retains the broader tooling.

## Design decision

Five directions were considered: a scaled desktop canvas, a horizontally scrolling desktop workspace, a list-first browser, a full-screen scene with modal controls, and a focused scene with compact controls and a readable record companion. The last direction best combines the existing visual identity with comfortable touch targets. The corridor uses a short step rail as an alternative to selecting small 3D cards. The plate keeps its physical well board and adds the shared 3D Sample history scene; a readable result companion sits below either view so details scroll naturally in both orientations.

This supersedes the original narrow-screen projection of the complete desktop shell. Desktop source and navigation remain the authority at widths of 1024 CSS pixels and above. Below that boundary, Corridor and Plate are the two persistent navigation choices. Corridor mounts immediately; Plate loads on first selection and stays mounted afterward. Switching retains lane, camera, well, result, lens, and scroll position. Crossing the desktop boundary retains Corridor / Plate, the selected well and result, and Plate overview / Sample history with its lens, spacing and depth. Desktop Plate arrives in the compact 3D view; a mobile-first visit retains its Well board choice when returning from desktop. Explicit Corridor selection becomes the shared destination. The receiving 3D scene fits its own canvas; rotation within the mobile range keeps the mounted camera. Accepted record and comparison edits, file restores, repair deadlines, successful history reads, environment and refusal notices also survive this boundary. Reload begins a fresh browser session.

Linked references open the exact source, instruction, artifact, comparison or decision in a readable companion; Back restores the originating record. Portrait and landscape are both functional. Rotation offers more space rather than an entry requirement. Browser zoom remains available outside the corridor and flat plate direct-manipulation canvases. Plate gestures belong to the scene; records and controls outside it retain ordinary page scrolling. Reduced-motion preferences remove camera transitions and smooth scrolling. Primary controls and card alternatives remain usable with keyboard focus.

## Source ownership

- `src/app/components/mobile/MobileShowcase.tsx` owns the two destinations, first-visit Plate loading, retained mobile views, and compact About dialog. `mobile-showcase.css` owns their layout.
- `MobileCorridor.tsx` and `mobile-corridor.css` own the touch projection, lane selection, camera controls, step rail and record dialog. `corridorView.ts` composes the lane, stage, kind and query filters before rendering. `MobileCorridorExplorer.tsx` and `MobileCorridorDetails.tsx` load when requested. `MobileReferenceRecord.tsx` is a separate lazy exact-ID receiver using `services/recordNavigation.ts`; it presents the selected record without loading desktop chapter code. `components/corridor/CorridorScene.tsx` owns the renderer and card palette shared with `views/LanesView.tsx`; `corridorCamera.ts` owns shared pitch bounds, continuous yaw and the three preset destinations. `useCorridorPresetMotion.ts` owns preset and Reset motion, replacement, direct-input cancellation and reduced-motion behavior; the renderer keeps direct input free of CSS transitions. Its pure `corridorProjection.ts` boundary resolves relationships from the supplied visible cards and lanes, so every painted arrow has two visible endpoints. The renderer receives canonical data through its callers.
- `MobilePlate.tsx` and `mobile-plate.css` own the board/3D choice, touch controls, story index and record companion. Its lazy `PlateHistoryProvider` and `PlateOverlayProvider` consume session-owned history and recovery state; environment and notifications live above the responsive workspace. Accepted actions survive both scene navigation and desktop/mobile replacement. `PlateScene3D` receives an explicit compact presentation and active flag. `PlateCameraControls` owns the shared fit, center, zoom and angle rail; `useProjectedCamera` keeps labels and the record thread aligned with manual exploration. `PlateLensLegend` projects the same paint vocabulary as the wells and discs. `ResultTray.tsx` shares record columns and write actions between presentations. `plateProjection.ts` supplies accepted file/query facts and posture summaries to both; `usePlateTouch.ts` reuses the two-contact gesture controller. `data/wellResults.ts`, `plate/stackLens.ts`, `plate/plateGeometry.ts`, and `services/dataAvailability.ts` retain data, color, geometry and policy meaning.
- `services/plateHistory` owns selected-well GraphQL reads, complete canonical decoding, fixed-clock agreement, request cancellation and successful-read cache lifetime. `plate/PlateHistorySource.tsx` and `plate-history.css` own the shared compact source rail. The successful-read Map is a lazy browser-session resource; in-flight cancellation remains with the requesting view. Accepted file overlays apply after fetched histories. Missing and empty successful reads remain distinct from an unavailable source with explicit example fallback.
- `main.tsx` installs one typed `ThemeProvider` above both workspaces. Its Appearance choice persists independently of scene and accepted-record state. `App.tsx` and `useCompactViewport.ts` select the receiving workspace. `DesktopWorkspace.tsx` contains the original desktop shell and providers behind a dynamic import. `components/DeferredView.tsx` owns loading and download-failure recovery. `index.html` allows safe-area layout without limiting user zoom.
- `services/exploration/ExplorationProvider.tsx` owns shared view intent above `App.tsx`'s responsive branch: scene, desktop Corridor controls, Plate options, selected well/result and compact Board/3D choice. Navigation, Corridor and Plate have separate context subscriptions. This retains the explored view while each workspace remains independently loadable; companion scroll and camera fitting retain their existing scene owners.
- `App.tsx` installs shared Environment and Notification providers plus `services/browserSessionContext.tsx` above responsive projection. `browserSession.ts` retains feature-factory values per app instance; immutable state cells expose stable subscription/snapshot methods. Deferred Record, Comparison and Plate providers keep their reducers and commands in their feature modules. Athena repair state records its completion deadline and notice context: a receiving Plate schedules only the remaining delay, and an overdue repair completes once on return. Timer cleanup stays local; rebuilds remain queued.
- `components/useLanesShortcuts.ts` maps desktop **C → Corridor** and **P → Plate** through that same navigation owner. The active, visible Lanes workspace owns the listener; a retained Lanes view releases it during an exact-record visit to another section. Input, textarea, select and editable content retain typing; modifiers, composition, repeated and already-handled key events retain their existing behavior. Compact touch navigation stays unchanged. The scene buttons carry small key hints and `aria-keyshortcuts`; Plate's existing U and Escape controls keep their separate history behavior.

## Corridor capability parity

The single-lane mobile entry remains the default. The additional controls replace the earlier single-lane-only scope; desktop composition remains unchanged.

| Desktop operation | Mobile receiver |
| --- | --- |
| Multi-lane and stage view | Overview, stage buttons and the Explore scope selector. |
| Hide, reveal and focus lanes | Explore → Lanes; Focus reveals and selects the named lane. The lane selector and previous/next rail remain direct paths. |
| Card-kind and text filtering, arrows | Explore → Search and Layers; one visible population feeds cards and cross-lane edges. |
| Flags, surface labels, connections and streams | Shared scene geometry plus Lane details and the Streams disclosure. |
| Drafting key and nested cards | Explore → Layers and drafting key; card companion retains nested labels and identities. |
| Travel and camera | Persistent camera controls, continuous yaw, top-weighted pitch and three spring presets. Scene gestures own only the canvas; record companions scroll normally. |
| Exact referenced records and return | Linked record buttons open source-owned readable fields and a Back action within the companion. Missing records show a bounded unavailable state. |

## Evidence-informed boundaries

The [WCAG target-size guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html) sets a 24px minimum with conditions; this composition uses larger primary targets and readable alternatives for small scene objects. The [dragging-movements guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html) supports separate tap controls for camera adjustments and direct record access. The [orientation guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/orientation.html) supports functional portrait and landscape views.

Native buttons, selectors, dialogs, and [disclosures](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/) carry state and keyboard behavior. [Touch-action](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/touch-action) is scoped to the corridor canvas so its pointer gestures coexist with native scrolling elsewhere. These references guide implementation; browser and device observations establish actual behavior.
